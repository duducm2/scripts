Mnemonic Study System

This system helps you memorize complex topics (e.g. Piano, English Grammar, Communication) by combining a Bestiary (beasts as concept holders) with a Memory Palace (unique geolocations). Each knowledge atom is anchored to a Beast on a Street, with one required Character anchor per Street and Actions to make recall vivid and durable.

Reading order: Read Quick Start and the workflow diagram once. Prompt templates live in studies/technique/prompts/; each prompt's Input (always last) section lists what to attach (lines starting with 📎).

For AI runs: When a prompt cites README §<Section Name>, that section under Canonical Rules is the single source of truth. Do not invent rules outside those anchors. Topic-specific concepts come from the video transcript and plan.md; the general framework and mnemonic rules live here—attach this README for Story Architect and Story Reduction; do not add a separate "theory" paste.

Canonical Rules

All prompts cite these sections by exact heading name (README §<Heading>). Rules MUST NOT be duplicated or contradicted elsewhere.

Knowledge Atom Structure

Definitions:

  • Knowledge Atom: The complete entry/record for a single concept.
  • Peg: Letters from the Bestiary as singles and pairs (e.g. A, AB). NEVER use numbers.
  • Knowledge / Definition: The rehearsal summary of the concept—the line the user reads to recall what the atom means. Always written as 💡 **Context:** …. NEVER confuse this with Quote.
  • Story/Action: The narrative event performed by the Beast.
  • Quote: Direct transcript excerpt from the video source. NEVER prefix Quote with 💡.

Sensory channel emojis (required beside every · sensory: marker on 🟧 and 🟦 lines; channel word first, then its emoji):

Channel Emoji
visual 👁️
auditory 👂
tactile ✋
olfactory 👃
gustatory 👅
thermal 🌡️

Format: · sensory: [channel] [emoji] (e.g. · sensory: visual 👁️). NEVER invent other sensory emojis; NEVER omit the emoji; NEVER place the emoji before the channel word.

Allowed emojis (Story Architect / Story Reduction output): Only 🟧 (topic/beast header), 🟦 (sub-atom), 💡 (definition / **Context:** line), and the sensory-channel emojis above—plus the fixed section title 🖼️ Mnemonic image prompts required by the output contract. NEVER invent any other emoji in beast headers, Context, Quote, Narrative, summaries, or image-prompt body text.

Structure per Knowledge Atom (separated by clear line breaks):

  1. Header: 🟧 [Peg Letters] [Beast Name] · sensory: [channel] [emoji] — required sensory channel for the main topic, with its mapped emoji from Sensory channel emojis above.
  2. Definition (Context): 💡 **Context:** [Knowledge Summary] — one-line rehearsal definition. MUST start with 💡. This is the main rehearsal element; it is NOT the Quote.
  3. Quote (Target Payload): Direct video quote, verbatim from the transcript. Place before Narrative so the target payload is reachable without traversing the story. NEVER use 💡 on Quote.
  4. Narrative (Story/Action): Short beat (2–4 sentences). MUST pass the Bizarreness Gate. Encode the atom's meaning through bizarre beast action, props, and (if smashed) zone accessories—the action is the mnemonic cue that triggers recall of the Quote. Narrative SHOULD enact the header's sensory channel. Use the street's single assigned character where it strengthens recall—NEVER add a second named character on the same street. Beast-to-beast interaction is optional (default none)—use it only when it strengthens the story or memory link; otherwise do not reference other beasts. NEVER require reading another beast's narrative to decode this one.

Retrieval path (dual-access): (1) Scan path: peg → 💡 **Context:** → Quote—target reached without Narrative. (2) Palace path: recall the beast's image and the encoding action → remember the Quote. Both paths are valid; Narrative is not a gate to the payload.

Smashed beast structure (after Story Reduction SMASH mode):

  1. Header: 🟧 [Peg Letters] [Beast Name] · sensory: [channel] [emoji] — peg identity and a baseline sensory channel for the beast body only (required; distinct from every 🟦 channel on this beast; emoji from Sensory channel emojis). The beast body is a neutral canvas: it MUST NOT encode any knowledge atom and MUST NOT carry a definition (💡 **Context:**) on the 🟧 level.
  2. Sub-atoms (Anatomical Micro-Loci): Up to 4 🟦 lines in Z1→Z4 order—every merged knowledge atom MUST be assigned to exactly one 🟦 zone. Each 🟦 line names its zone, active anatomical interaction, brief knowledge hook, and sensory channel with emoji (e.g. · sensory: visual 👁️). Immediately under each 🟦 line, emit that atom's rehearsal definition as 💡 **Context:** …. Do not use a combined group-level Context under the 🟧 header.
  3. Quote(s): One verbatim video quote per merged atom, in source order—before Narrative. NEVER prefix Quote with 💡.
  4. Narrative: One active beat per assigned zone, matching the 🟦 lines—each zone encodes that sub-atom's meaning through mechanical engagement with its accessory. The beast body's overall posture or presence MUST NOT encode knowledge; only the zone beats do. Self-contained beat; optional cross-beast links only when they serve the concept, transcript logic, or a stronger mnemonic—otherwise do not reference other beasts.

Example (single-atom beast):

🟧 **[B] [bird of paradise]** · sensory: visual 👁️

💡 **Context:** The past perfect combines had with a past participle.

**Quote:** "The past perfect uses had plus the past participle."

**Narrative:** A microscopic bird of paradise hammers a skyscraper-sized chalkboard wedged into the gate post; each peck freezes solid neon grammar dust in mid-air above the chalk surface.

Output quality: Format optimized for both text reading and Read Aloud (TTS). Maintain strict visual separation (line breaks) between every Knowledge Atom.

Streets and Depth Slots

Locations taken from your hometown, e.g. using Google Maps.

  • Crucial constraint: Maximum 5 Beasts per Street. NEVER place more than 5 Beasts on any single street.
  • Shared street background (visual only): All beasts on a street composite into the same Google Maps background—but each beast occupies a distinct depth slot with a structural anchor within that image.
  • Street size target: Prefer exactly 5 beasts per street. The final street of a topic MAY have fewer if the topic ends before reaching five.
  • Streets are dynamically assigned per study topic; the Story Generator MUST state how many streets the topic requires (Total streets required: N).
  • A later continuous session MAY add more beasts to a street until it reaches five; when the topic needs more capacity, add new streets in that same flow.
  • Character anchor (required): Exactly one character from characters.json anchors each street—see Characters.
  • Maps capture tip (user): Prefer Street View locations with visible depth cues—facades, roofs, gates, corners—not flat homogeneous pavement filling the frame.

Depth slots (Fan Effect mitigation):

Five beasts on one street is high-density loci (near working-memory limits). Similar backgrounds (e.g. all pavement) cause proactive interference and Fan Effect slowdown. Mitigation: mandatory depth slots—fixed Z-axis + lateral coordinates—without reducing the 5-beast target or Google Maps backgrounds.

Beast order on street Depth slot Placement rule
1st ForegroundLeft Near camera, left third; anchor to a distinct structure (gate, wall, vehicle)—not open pavement alone
2nd MidgroundRight Mid depth, right third; distinct structural anchor
3rd BackgroundCenter Far depth, center line; facade, end of street, or distant landmark
4th Aerial Elevated: roof, wall top, lamp post, tree branch, balcony
5th ForegroundRight Near camera, right third; distinct structural anchor
  • One beast per depth slot on a street; NEVER two beasts in the same slot.
  • Fewer than 5 beasts: use the first N slots in order.
  • Structural anchor required: bind each beast to a named feature in the Maps scene (roof, gate, bench, lamp post, parked car, corner)—not "on the sidewalk" or "on pavement" alone when a structure exists in frame.
  • Image prompts MUST state Depth slot: + structural anchor per beast.

Independent Beast Beats

  • Each beast is a self-contained retrieval unit. Its Narrative encodes only its knowledge atom (and, if smashed, its own 🟦 sub-atoms via accessories). The street does not share a narrative thread, shared props, or cross-beast story links.
  • Any-order recall: Random access works because each beat is isolated at its depth slot—not because the user reconstructs sibling beasts via shared props, environmental cues, or thematic echoes.
  • No fixed chain language: NEVER imply a required sequence like "next", "then", "after this beast", or "Beast 1 → Beast 2 → …".
  • Beast-to-beast interaction (optional, default none): Direct links between beasts are allowed only when they serve the concept, transcript logic, or a stronger mnemonic. Do not add generic references to other beasts on the street (e.g. shouting, bleating, or waving at a nearby beast) when there is no meaningful link. NEVER require reading another beast's narrative to decode this one.

Bizarreness Gate

LLMs default to plausible, coherent scenes. Memory athletes need the improbable. Every Knowledge Atom Narrative MUST pass this gate—mundane associations decay; bizarre ones stick.

Each Narrative MUST include at least one improbability lever wrapping the encoding action:

Lever Rule Example
Physics violation Impossible motion, matter, or causality Beast phases through the gate while freezing solid neon grammar dust mid-air
Disproportionate scale Beast or prop drastically wrong size vs the street Microscopic elephant perched on a gate post; skyscraper-tall ant straddling the facade
Sensory juxtaposition Clash two senses in one beat Deafening silence visible as ripples; chalkboard dust that tastes like thunder
  • Reject mundane defaults: If the beat could pass as a normal documentary caption, rewrite until at least one lever is obvious.
  • Image prompts: Carry the same lever into foreground description (state scale or surreal detail explicitly when used).
  • SMASH sub-atoms: Each 🟦 zone interaction SHOULD also be bizarre or physically impossible—not a passive prop.

Actions palette

How you imagine interacting with a Beast. A Character MAY perform the interaction in the scene instead of you. Use:

  • Tangible — touch, weight, texture
  • Grotesque — exaggerated, bizarre, unsettling, but non-graphic
  • Sensual — adult-safe, non-explicit tension, attraction when it materially improves recall

  • Impactful — loud, dramatic, startling, high-stakes without graphic harm

These are the tools for passing the Bizarreness Gate—not optional decoration on easy beats.

Anatomical Micro-Loci

When the palace has too many beasts or streets, run Story Reduction in SMASH mode to merge similar or related knowledge atoms into one beast (schema blending) instead of giving each atom its own peg.

  • One beast, up to four sub-atoms: Each merged atom becomes one 🟦 subtopic line (maximum 4 per beast; merge groups with more than 4 pegs MUST split into multiple smashed beasts). All knowledge atoms live on 🟦 lines plus their paired 💡 **Context:** definitions—never on the beast body itself.
  • Neutral beast body: The 🟧 header names the peg and a baseline sensory channel for the beast's body presence only. The beast body MUST NOT encode any knowledge atom and MUST NOT carry 💡 **Context:**; it is a neutral canvas that holds zone accessories.
  • Encode nuances on zone anatomy: Alter features, expression, posture, and zone-specific anatomy via 🟦 accessories—not by encoding facts on the whole body, and not by adding extra beasts.
  • Definition per sub-atom: Immediately under every 🟦 line, emit 💡 **Context:** [that atom's knowledge summary] for rehearsal. The 🟦 line is the zone/accessory mnemonic cue; the 💡 **Context:** line is the definition. NEVER put 💡 on Quote or Narrative.
  • Image prompts: Story Reduction outputs long, detailed foreground prompts so each accessory renders in its assigned anatomical zone with active engagement on the beast. Do not invent a separate "main topic" action on the beast body.

Language & IPA

Default to simple English everywhere you write (Context, Narrative, image prompts, quality summary): short sentences, everyday words, and concrete images. Write as if explaining to a curious teenager—not an academic paper.

Banned in your own prose (unless inside a preserved quote): Rare or show-off vocabulary; Latin/French roots where a common Anglo-Saxon word works; idioms that need a dictionary; discipline jargon, acronyms, and technical terms unless the transcript quote requires them—and even then, prefer wrapping the idea in plain language in Context/Narrative and keep the exact term only in the Quote when needed.

Bestiary Shifting Logic

After Story Reduction (SMASH or REMOVE), the peg sequence MUST be an unbroken alphabetical mnemonic. No gaps.

  • Rule: For each remaining beast, in narrative order, assign the next consecutive peg from the bestiary (A, B, C, …). Update the Beast name to the beast for that new peg (from bestiary.json).
  • Example: Atoms at pegs A, B, C, D, E. User removes B and D. Remaining (A, C, E) → re-pegged as A, B, C with beasts from pegs A, B, C.
  • Apply this shift to the entire story: every Header, and every in-narrative reference to a Beast or peg, MUST use the new peg and Beast name.
  • Sequence continues A, B, C, … (or A, B, …, Z, Aa, …) with no gaps.

Memorization Techniques

Cognitive Architecture and Mnemonic Mastery: An Exhaustive Analysis of Elite Memory Techniques and Digital Pedagogical MediaThe human memory is frequently perceived as a static, genetic endowment—a biological hard drive with fixed capacity and highly variable reliability. Throughout modern educational systems, an individual’s ability to recall information is often treated as an immutable trait, leading to the widespread cultural myth of the innate "photographic" or eidetic memory. However, the emergence of "mental athletes" and the proliferation of competitive memory sports over the last three decades have definitively demonstrated that extraordinary recall is rarely the result of a genetic anomaly. Instead, it is the product of rigorous cognitive training, the implementation of specialized mnemonic frameworks, and the deliberate, algorithmic manipulation of the brain's neurobiological predispositions.Over the past decade, the democratization of these elite cognitive techniques has accelerated exponentially through digital media. Video-sharing platforms, most notably YouTube, have become the primary pedagogical vectors for mnemonic instruction, allowing world-class memory champions to translate ancient, abstract cognitive frameworks into highly accessible visual tutorials. Through comprehensive digital demonstrations, figures such as Joshua Foer, Alex Mullen, Nelson Dellis, Alberto Dell'Isola, and Renato Alves have deconstructed the myth of the innate genius, proving that superhuman recall is achievable through systematic methodologies.This report provides an exhaustive, deep-dive investigation into the cognitive mechanisms, structural frameworks, historical contexts, and pedagogical methodologies utilized by world-class memory champions. By systematically analyzing the techniques demonstrated within prominent instructional media, this document articulates precisely how abstract, meaningless data is translated into rich, unforgettable cognitive architecture, and how these systems are taught to the global public.The Neurocognitive Foundations of Mnemonic SystemsAt the core of all advanced mnemonic techniques is a fundamental physiological reality regarding human cognitive evolution: the human brain is not optimized for remembering abstract symbols, disconnected numerical strings, or arbitrary semantic data. Throughout the vast majority of human evolutionary history, survival depended heavily on spatial navigation, environmental awareness, and visual recognition. Consequently, the brain's visual and spatial memory centers are highly developed and remarkably robust, while the capacity for the rote memorization of abstract linguistic or mathematical data remains severely limited. Elite memory athletes do not possess larger brains; rather, they bypass these inherent biological limitations by exploiting the brain's natural neuroplasticity through an active cognitive process known as elaborative encoding.Elaborative Encoding and the Depth of ProcessingElaborative encoding involves taking information that lacks inherent meaning or context and transforming it into a format that connects deeply with existing neural networks and semantic memories. Joshua Foer, a science journalist who embedded himself in the subculture of memory sports and subsequently trained his own memory to win the 2006 USA Memory Championship, illustrates this physiological concept through the well-known "Baker/baker paradox".When a test subject is told that a stranger's last name is "Baker," the information is entirely abstract. The name "Baker" is tethered to no visual context, no sensory input, and no emotional resonance; it is an arbitrary linguistic tag. However, when a subject is told that a stranger's profession is a "baker," the brain immediately activates a vast, pre-existing network of multisensory associations. The subject subconsciously visualizes the white uniform and chef's hat, imagines the smell of fresh bread, and recalls the tactile texture of flour. The word is identical, but the depth of cognitive processing is entirely different.Elaborative encoding actively forces the brain to transform the abstract, unmoored name "Baker" into the sensory-rich, highly contextualized image of a baker, thereby ensuring long-term retention. This depth of processing requires acute focus and a state of deliberate mindfulness. Foer argues philosophically that everyday forgetfulness—such as misplacing car keys or forgetting a colleague's name—is rarely a failure of memory capacity, but rather a profound failure of attention and processing. Modern reliance on external cognitive prosthetics, such as smartphones and digital calendars, has eroded the habit of processing information deeply, leading to a phenomenon of digital amnesia. Drawing inspiration from EP, an amnesic patient who suffered total memory loss, Foer postulates that "our lives are the sum of our memories," and that relying on external devices strips individuals of their fundamental capacity to live a memorable life.Neural Correlates of Memory FormationThe efficacy of elaborative encoding and subsequent mnemonic techniques is deeply rooted in measurable neurobiology. The process of converting short-term stimuli into permanent, long-term retention relies upon the synchronized activation of several key brain regions, most notably the hippocampus, the visual cortex, and the amygdala.The visual cortex is intensely engaged when memory athletes translate abstract data into vivid, exaggerated mental pictures, essentially tricking the brain into processing an imagined visual as a real memory. The hippocampus, a region critical for spatial memory, mapping, and navigation, is heavily utilized when athletes place these visual images into structured mental environments. Finally, the amygdala, which is responsible for emotional processing and the assignment of significance to stimuli, is activated through the deliberate manipulation of the imagined visuals.Mnemonic systems explicitly require practitioners to create bizarre, humorous, violent, or sexually explicit mental images. The brain is biologically programmed to ignore the mundane; a normal apple sitting on a counter will not be retained by the brain because it poses no threat and offers no novelty. However, if the practitioner imagines a giant, anthropomorphic apple screaming while being smashed with a hammer, the amygdala is activated by the novelty and perceived emotional intensity of the bizarre image. The more unusual or emotionally stimulating the mental image, the more effectively the amygdala tags the memory for long-term storage, overriding the brain's natural tendency to discard irrelevant information.The clinical applications of these neurobiological principles further validate their efficacy beyond the realm of competitive sports. Dr. Nancy Chiaravalloti, the Director of Neuropsychology and Neuroscience at the Kessler Foundation, has conducted extensive research demonstrating that techniques emphasizing structured context and rich mental imagery are highly effective for cognitive rehabilitation. By utilizing a structured treatment protocol known as the modified Story Memory Technique (mSMT), researchers have successfully improved the learning and memory capacities of healthy individuals, as well as patients suffering from debilitating neurological conditions such as Multiple Sclerosis and Traumatic Brain Injury.The Method of Loci: Spatial Architecture as a Mnemonic SubstrateThe most prominent, historically significant, and universally utilized technique among memory champions is the "Memory Palace," formally known in academic literature as the Method of Loci. The origins of this technique are deeply rooted in antiquity, supposedly invented by the Greek poet Simonides of Ceos approximately 2,500 years ago.As Brazilian memory champion Alberto Dell'Isola explains in his instructional media, ancient civilizations were highly dependent on oral traditions. Before the invention of the Gutenberg printing press, the mass production of texts was impossible, necessitating that scholars, religious figures, and orators memorize vast quantities of information, including entire sacred texts and hours-long speeches. Roman orators, most notably Cicero, utilized the Method of Loci to memorize legal arguments and philosophical treatises topic by topic, recognizing that attempting to memorize a speech word-for-word was highly susceptible to catastrophic failure if a single connecting word was forgotten.The Method of Loci operates by fully co-opting the brain's exceptional spatial navigation capabilities. It requires the practitioner to visualize a highly familiar physical environment—an edifice—and populate it with the bizarre, elaboratively encoded visual representations of the data they wish to remember.Structural Mechanics of the Memory PalaceThe construction and utilization of a Memory Palace follow a precise, algorithmic methodology. Through the analysis of highly structured digital tutorials provided by world champions Alex Mullen, Nelson Dellis, and Alberto Dell'Isola, a universal standard operating procedure for the creation of spatial memory architectures emerges.The first step requires the selection of the foundational edifice. The chosen location must be intimately familiar to the practitioner, requiring zero cognitive effort to recall. While real environments such as a childhood home, a current apartment, a workplace, or a daily commute route are most common, the environment can also be semi-real (such as a frequently watched television show set like the office from The Office) or entirely fictional (such as a digitally rendered map from a highly played video game). The sole critical requirement is the practitioner's ability to navigate the space effortlessly and consistently in the "mind's eye".The second step is the establishment of a rigid, linear route through the chosen environment. To ensure that information is recalled in the correct sequence, the practitioner must establish a strict, unchanging path. In his instructional series, Alex Mullen recommends a highly structured approach: navigating the edifice room by room, and consistently moving through each room in a counter-clockwise direction to prevent any spatial confusion or skipped data points.The third step involves the identification of loci, or specific storage stations, within the environment. Along the established route, the practitioner must designate distinct spots where mental images will be "deposited." Mullen advises a standardized approach of selecting exactly five distinct loci per room. Crucially, these loci must feature variety in their height, texture, and appearance so that the locations do not visually blur together in the practitioner's mind. In a demonstration utilizing a kitchen, Mullen selects a sink, a set of lower cabinets, a tabletop, a countertop kitchen mixer, and an oven as his five sequential loci.The fourth step is the actual deposition of vivid imagery. The practitioner translates the target data into absurd, unforgettable images and mentally anchors them to the designated loci. Nelson Dellis demonstrates this practical application using a standard grocery list. Rather than attempting to rote-memorize the words "apples, eggs, bread, and milk," Dellis visualizes the items interacting aggressively with the environment: he imagines apples smashed violently across the floor of his apartment, eggs cracked and dripping over a hallway mirror, and thick rolls of toilet paper stuffed into a bathtub.The final step is mental traversal and retrieval. When the stored information is required—for instance, when the practitioner arrives at the grocery store—they simply close their eyes and mentally re-walk the established route through their apartment. Because the spatial architecture remains stable, the bizarre, emotionally charged images left at each station are easily "picked up," allowing for flawless sequential recall. Nested Memory Palaces

Nested Memory Palaces (The Portal / Bag Method)

Objective

To store and organize massive quantities of information (e.g., the entire German alphabet, 66 grammar rules, 50 phrasal verbs) without violating the strict "5 Beasts per Street" limit and preventing cognitive overload (the "Fan Effect").

The Concept

This technique relies on creating a Macro-Level Syllabus (or Index) where a single Beast acts as a "bag," a gateway, or a portal that contains a massive sub-list of information. In cognitive science and memory sports, this is known as the Nested Memory Palace or Russian Doll Method.

Instead of trying to attach 50 separate facts to a single Beast (which causes severe retroactive interference and cue overload), the Beast merely holds the concept of the category. Interacting with that Beast "teleports" you to a completely separate set of streets dedicated to that topic.

The Two-Dimensional Structure

Dimension 1: The Index Bag (Macro-Level)

Create a high-level street (a "Syllabus Palace"). Here, each Beast holds a giant container (a bag, a ledger, a portal) representing an entire massive topic. * [A] Arachne weaves a glowing alphabet web that acts as a bag. (Points to the German Alphabet). * [B] Bird of Paradise holds a massive, 66-page ledger in its beak. (Points to 66 Rules of Gerunds and Infinitives). * [C] Cat is tangled in a squirming sack of 50 distinct verbs. (Points to 50 Phrasal Verbs).

Purpose: This street is solely for macro-rehearsal. When you walk this street, you are reminding your brain of the broad categories you are responsible for.

Dimension 2: The Content (Micro-Level)

When you mentally "open the bag" or enter the portal, you are teleported to a completely separate set of streets. Inside this "Gerund Realm", you restart your Bestiary sequence (A, B, C...) and expand the 66 rules safely across 14 separate streets (adhering strictly to the 5 beasts per street limit).

Rehearsal Strategy

  • Macro-Review (Quick Scan): If you only have 5 minutes, walk your Index Street. Acknowledge Arachne, Bird of Paradise, and the Cat. Touch the bags, remember the topic they hold, and move on.
  • Micro-Review (Deep Dive): For a dedicated study session, walk up to the Cat and mentally step "inside" its bag. Reset your mental space and walk down the 10 separate, expanded streets where 50 beasts are interacting with specific phrasal verbs.

## Procedure

### 1. Watch and extract

- Process the entire video. Do not skip sections.
- Extract every distinct concept, definition, rule, and example. Do not omit significant details.
- Remove repetition (repeated phrases, filler, redundant restatements), but strictly preserve **content depth** so downstream story generation has full semantic material.

### 2. Preserve verbatim sentences

- For each key concept or rule, include at least one **exact quote** or near-verbatim sentence from the video.
- Preserve grammar examples, formula statements, and rule statements exactly as spoken or shown on screen.
- Quotes MUST match transcript audio or on-screen text word-for-word. NEVER paraphrase, "clean up", or shorten a quote.

### 3. Note visual and auditory cues

- Where the video reinforces a concept with a visual (on-screen text, diagram, instructor gesture) or auditory (emphasis, tone shift, repetition), add a `**Visual/auditory cue:**` line under that concept.
- These cues let the downstream Architect wire mnemonic beats to concrete video moments.

### 4. Language handling

- **English videos:** transcribe in English.
- **Non-English videos:** transcribe in the source language. For each concept, additionally add an **English gloss** in parentheses after the verbatim source-language quote so the Architect can work in either language.
- **German content (any video containing German words or phrases):** whenever a German word or short fixed German phrase (about four words or fewer) appears in your **authored prose** (overview, concept explanations, cue lines), append IPA in **square brackets** immediately following, with a single space before the opening bracket. Use standard German (Hochdeutsch) IPA, broad transcription, with a stress mark ˈ before the stressed syllable.
  - Example: `The sign reads verboten [fɛɐ̯ˈboːtn̩].`
  - Example (short phrase): `Guten Morgen [ˈɡuːtn̩ ˈmɔʁɡn̩].`
- **Inside verbatim quotes:** NEVER insert IPA into the quoted characters (quotes stay identical to source). Instead, immediately **after** the quote line, add one line starting with `IPA:` listing every distinct German word or short phrase from that quote in the same bracket style, comma-separated if several.
  - Example line: `IPA: verboten [fɛɐ̯ˈboːtn̩], Schadenfreude [ˈʃaːdn̩ˌfʁɔʏdə]`

### 5. Style

- Polished, cohesive, concise, coherent. Full sentences where they help clarity.
- Plain vocabulary. Prefer short everyday words in your own prose; keep discipline jargon only when it appears inside a preserved quote.
- Optimize for **downstream reuse**: the Story Architect will scan your `>` blockquotes and your `## Exact sentences for story reuse` list to build mnemonic beats. Make both scannable.

- NEVER paraphrase a video sentence and label it as a quote.
