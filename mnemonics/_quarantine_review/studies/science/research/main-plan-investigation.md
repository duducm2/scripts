# Comprehensive Autonomous Scientific Researcher Study Plan

## 📃 Backlog
## Plan overview

The development of a highly proficient scientific researcher requires far more than the passive accumulation of domain-specific facts; it necessitates the systematic construction of an epistemological, methodological, and communicative framework. This curriculum is designed as a highly structured, progressively sequenced plan for self-directed learners aiming to transition from consumers of scientific literature to independent, rigorous producers of novel knowledge.

By adhering to this strict chronological design, the learner avoids the structural deficiencies of contemporary training. Every developmental milestone provides exactly two components: the specific theoretical or operational **Topic** to be mastered, and the single most reliable, comprehensive **Source** to execute that mastery, prioritizing authoritative video curricula that simulate graduate-level instruction.

## Phase 1: Foundational Epistemology and Literature Mastery

The primary objective of this initial phase is to establish the theoretical underpinnings of scientific inquiry and to develop the capacity to navigate, organize, and synthesize the vast corpus of existing human knowledge.

### Months 1–4: Epistemology and the Information Architecture of Science

**1. The Philosophy of Science and Epistemological Frameworks**

- [ ] 🟧 Understand the demarcation problem and criteria separating science from pseudoscience.
- [ ] 🟧 Study naturalism, empirical thought (Thales to Darwin), and Kuhnian paradigm shifts.
- [ ] 🟧 Master Popperian falsificationism and design testable null hypotheses.
- [ ] 🟧 Distinguish Bayesian updating from frequentist inference for later statistical work.
- [ ] 🟧 Internalize that scientific models are provisional, not absolute truth.

| Epistemological framework | Core principle                                                              | Implication for research design                                  |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Inductivism               | Generalizing from specific observations to universal laws                   | Prone to black swan fallacy; cannot definitively prove causality |
| Falsificationism (Popper) | Science progresses by proving hypotheses false, not true                    | Demands highly risky, testable null hypotheses                   |
| Paradigm shifts (Kuhn)    | Normal science operates within a framework until anomalies force revolution | Recognize when underlying assumptions are failing                |

**Source:**

- [Alexander Koryagin: Philosophy of Science, Introduction \| Course Overview](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Alexander+Koryagin+Philosophy+of+Science+Introduction+Course+Overview)

**2. Advanced Literature Review and Gap Identification**

- [ ] 🟧 Execute systematic literature reviews using PICO, SPICE, SPIDER, and PCC frameworks.
- [ ] 🟧 Construct Boolean search strings with nested operators, truncation, and database syntax.
- [ ] 🟧 Apply forward and backward citation snowballing on seminal papers.
- [ ] 🟧 Use Litmaps or Research Rabbit to map research silos and identify gaps.
- [ ] 🟧 Include grey literature to mitigate publication bias.

| Search framework | Acronym expansion                                                 | Primary use case                           |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| PICO             | Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome                     | Clinical trials and medical interventions  |
| SPICE            | Setting, Perspective, Intervention, Comparison, Evaluation        | Qualitative research and social sciences   |
| SPIDER           | Sample, Phenomenon of Interest, Design, Evaluation, Research type | Mixed-methods and phenomenological studies |

**Source:**

- [Andy Stapleton: How To Do A Literature Review (STRESS-FREE!)](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Andy+Stapleton+How+To+Do+A+Literature+Review+STRESS-FREE)

**3. Reference Management and Bibliographic Frameworks**

- [ ] 🟧 Master Zotero as the central reference workflow (not ad-hoc folders).
- [ ] 🟧 Extract and normalize bibliographic metadata via browser extensions.
- [ ] 🟧 Build tagging taxonomies for cross-disciplinary thematic search.
- [ ] 🟧 Configure sync, group libraries, and word-processor citation plugins.
- [ ] 🟧 Explore AI plugins within Zotero for rapid PDF extraction.

| Zotero functionality       | Operational benefit                            | Implementation mechanism                              |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Metadata parsing           | Eliminates manual entry and formatting errors  | Browser extension integration with academic databases |
| Tagging taxonomies         | Non-linear thematic search across disciplines  | Standardized nomenclature on item records             |
| Word processor integration | Automates in-text citations and bibliographies | Plugins for Microsoft Word or LibreOffice             |

**Source:**

- [Andy Stapleton: How to use Zotero's full potential](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Andy+Stapleton+How+to+use+Zotero+full+potential)

## Phase 2: Methodological Design and Statistical Rigor

In this phase, the learner shifts from understanding existing knowledge to designing the mechanisms required to generate new, reproducible data.

### Months 5–12: From Study Architecture to Programmatic Analysis

**4. Fundamentals of Qualitative Research Methods**

- [ ] 🟧 Differentiate ethnography, grounded theory, phenomenology, and participatory action research.
- [ ] 🟧 Formulate qualitative questions focused on "how" and "why," not "how many."
- [ ] 🟧 Design semi-structured interviews, focus groups, and ethnographic observations.
- [ ] 🟧 Apply thematic analysis, theoretical saturation, and iterative coding (e.g., ATLAS.ti).
- [ ] 🟧 Establish rigor via triangulation, member checking, and thick description.
- [ ] 🟧 Address positionality and reflexivity as the researcher is the primary instrument.

| Qualitative paradigm | Core objective                                                | Primary data collection method                     |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Phenomenology        | Understanding the lived experience of a phenomenon            | In-depth, unstructured interviews                  |
| Grounded theory      | Generating theory directly from data without prior hypotheses | Iterative coding and constant comparative analysis |
| Ethnography          | Understanding culture and social mechanics of a group         | Prolonged participant observation                  |

**Source:**

- [Yale University (Leslie Curry): Fundamentals of Qualitative Research Methods Playlist](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Yale+Leslie+Curry+Fundamentals+of+Qualitative+Research+Methods)

**5. Quantitative Research and Study Design Architecture**

- [ ] 🟧 Master cross-sectional, case-control, cohort, and RCT designs and temporal orientation.
- [ ] 🟧 Identify and mitigate selection, recall, survivorship bias, and confounding.
- [ ] 🟧 Apply randomization, blinding (single/double/triple), and control groups.
- [ ] 🟧 Match study design to research question, ethics, and funding constraints.

| Study design                | Temporal orientation         | Primary strength                                    |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Cross-sectional             | Single point in time         | Rapid prevalence assessment; hypothesis generation  |
| Case-control                | Retrospective                | Efficient for rare diseases or outcomes             |
| Cohort study                | Prospective or retrospective | Establishes temporality; calculates incidence rates |
| Randomized controlled trial | Prospective                  | Gold standard for establishing causality            |

**Source:**

- [Global Health with Greg Martin: Research Methods - Introduction](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Global+Health+Greg+Martin+Research+Methods+Introduction)

**6. Core Statistical Concepts and Probability Theory**

- [ ] 🟧 Internalize Normal, Binomial, and Poisson distributions and the Central Limit Theorem.
- [ ] 🟧 Understand NHST and what a p-value does and does not represent.
- [ ] 🟧 Interpret confidence intervals and Type I / Type II errors.
- [ ] 🟧 Conduct a priori power analyses before data collection.
- [ ] 🟧 Apply t-tests, ANOVA, standard error, R-squared, and regression foundations.

| Statistical concept | Definition                                                | Common misinterpretation                           |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| p-value             | Probability of observed data given a true null hypothesis | Falsely taken as probability the null is false     |
| Type I error        | Rejecting a true null (false positive)                    | Exacerbated by multiple testing without correction |
| Statistical power   | Probability of correctly rejecting a false null           | Ignored in underpowered studies                    |

**Source:**

- [StatQuest with Josh Starmer: Statistics Fundamentals Playlist](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=StatQuest+Josh+Starmer+Statistics+Fundamentals+Playlist)

**7. Applied Biostatistics and R Programming**

- [ ] 🟧 Work in R (vectors, matrices, data frames) instead of opaque point-and-click GUIs.
- [ ] 🟧 Wrangle, clean, and reshape messy datasets into tidy analysis-ready formats.
- [ ] 🟧 Implement linear, logistic, and Poisson regression with correct interpretation.
- [ ] 🟧 Use offset terms in Poisson models for aggregated rate data over time/population.
- [ ] 🟧 Produce survival analyses and publication-quality visualizations in scripted pipelines.

| Regression model in R | Dependent variable type | Example application                           |
| --------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Linear regression     | Continuous              | Predicting blood pressure from age and weight |
| Logistic regression   | Binary / dichotomous    | Predicting disease presence (yes/no)          |
| Poisson regression    | Count / rate data       | Modeling mutations over a time interval       |

**Source:**

- [MarinStatsLectures: R Programming & Statistics Full Tutorial Series](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=MarinStatsLectures+R+Programming+Statistics+Full+Tutorial+Series)

## Phase 3: Data Integrity, Ethics, and Management

With the ability to generate and analyze complex data secured, the researcher must learn how to properly steward that data.

### Months 13–16: Stewardship, Compliance, and Open Science

**8. FAIR Data Principles and Open Science**

- [ ] 🟧 Apply FAIR: Findable (DOIs, metadata), Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable.
- [ ] 🟧 Draft Data Management Plans required by major funding bodies.
- [ ] 🟧 Differentiate FAIR data from open data when privacy or IP constraints apply.
- [ ] 🟧 Prefer standard, non-proprietary formats (e.g., CSV over Excel).

| FAIR principle | Implementation strategy                                | Impact on scientific ecosystem                   |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Findable       | Persistent identifiers (DOIs) and robust metadata      | Prevents data loss; enables algorithmic indexing |
| Accessible     | Trusted repositories with clear access protocols       | Long-term retrieval after researcher departure   |
| Interoperable  | Standard, non-proprietary file formats                 | Cross-disciplinary meta-analyses and integration |
| Reusable       | Clear licenses (e.g., Creative Commons) and provenance | Protects IP while granting reuse permissions     |

**Source:**

- [Utrecht University: Is my data FAIR? \| Research Data management](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Utrecht+University+Is+my+data+FAIR+Research+Data+management)

**9. Research Ethics and Scientific Integrity**

- [ ] 🟧 Study Nuremberg Code, Declaration of Helsinki, and Belmont Report foundations.
- [ ] 🟧 Navigate IRB protocols, informed consent, and vulnerable-population protections.
- [ ] 🟧 Recognize misconduct: fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, image manipulation.
- [ ] 🟧 Avoid DRPs: p-hacking, HARKing, selective reporting, inappropriate authorship.
- [ ] 🟧 Operate within ORI and institutional compliance standards.

| Ethical violation category | Definition                                | Preventative measure                                                  |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Research misconduct        | Fabrication, falsification, or plagiarism | Institutional oversight, raw data auditing, whistleblower protections |
| P-hacking (DRP)            | Testing until p < 0.05                    | Pre-registration and analysis plans                                   |
| HARKing (DRP)              | Post hoc hypothesis presented as a priori | Separate exploratory from confirmatory analysis                       |

**Source:**

- [NIH VideoCast: IPPCR Ethics in the Conduct of Research (James L. Gulley)](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=NIH+IPPCR+Ethics+in+the+Conduct+of+Research+James+Gulley)

## Phase 4: Scientific Writing, Publication, and Dissemination

This phase is dedicated to academic writing and verbal presentation—structuring arguments for reviewers and readers.

### Months 17–24: Manuscript Architecture and Rhetorical Precision

**10. The IMRaD Framework and Manuscript Structuring**

- [ ] 🟧 Draft Introduction as a funnel: context → gap → research question/hypothesis.
- [ ] 🟧 Write Methods as a replicable recipe with collection and statistical detail.
- [ ] 🟧 Report Results objectively with tables and figures, no interpretation.
- [ ] 🟧 Structure Discussion as inverted funnel: interpretation, limitations, broader impact.
- [ ] 🟧 Craft concise abstracts as the primary entry point for readers.

| Manuscript section | Structural metaphor | Primary objective                                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Introduction       | The funnel          | Establish gap and state hypothesis                    |
| Methods            | The recipe          | Transparent, reproducible procedures                  |
| Results            | The ledger          | Objective numerical reporting                         |
| Discussion         | The inverted funnel | Interpret findings, admit limitations, broader impact |

**Source:**

- [SAGES: IMRAD Structure Part I & II](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=SAGES+IMRAD+Structure+Part+I+II)

**11. Advanced Scientific Writing and Stylistic Nuance**

- [ ] 🟧 Eliminate nominalizations; prefer active voice over passive.
- [ ] 🟧 Place known information at sentence start and new information at the end.
- [ ] 🟧 Self-edit for clutter, redundancy, and filler phrases.
- [ ] 🟧 Optimize signal-to-noise so reviewers evaluate science, not grammar.

| Stylistic error     | Example of poor usage                                               | Optimized revision                    |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Nominalization      | An investigation of the data was performed by the team              | The team investigated the data        |
| Passive voice abuse | The reagents were added to the solution                             | We added the reagents to the solution |
| Syntactic clutter   | It is a fact that due to the fact that it rained, the trial stopped | The trial stopped because it rained   |

**Source:**

- [Stanford University / Kristin Sainani: Writing in the Sciences (Full Course Playlist)](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Kristin+Sainani+Writing+in+the+Sciences+Stanford)

**12. Delivering High-Impact Scientific Presentations**

- [ ] 🟧 Apply cognitive load theory: minimal slide text, visual schematics, no verbatim reading.
- [ ] 🟧 Structure talks as mystery-to-resolution narrative arcs, not lab chronology.
- [ ] 🟧 Manage anxiety, pacing, and stage presence for conferences and defenses.
- [ ] 🟧 Handle Q&A without defensiveness; acknowledge limits and future work.

| Presentation component | Common academic mistake               | Optimized strategy                             |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Slide design           | Dense bullets read verbatim           | Visual schematics; text anchors key terms only |
| Narrative structure    | Chronological lab failures            | Curated mystery-to-resolution arc              |
| Q&A handling           | Defensiveness on methodological flaws | Acknowledge limits; redirect to future studies |

**Source:**

- [MIT CompBio (Manolis Kellis): Lecture 25 - How to Present](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=MIT+Manolis+Kellis+Lecture+25+How+to+Present)

## Phase 5: Funding, Grantsmanship, and Independence

The final phase decodes federal funding and teaches how to secure capital for an independent research agenda.

### Months 25–36: The Architecture of Federal Funding and Strategic Proposal Design

**13. Navigating the Grant Application Process**

- [ ] 🟧 Navigate NIH funding databases, NOFOs, and institute strategic priorities.
- [ ] 🟧 Differentiate F-series (training), K-series (career development), and R-series (research) mechanisms.
- [ ] 🟧 Understand peer review: study sections, reviewer assignment, Significance/Innovation/Approach scoring.
- [ ] 🟧 Use RePORT and pre-award systems to map funded grants and the landscape.

| NIH grant mechanism       | Target applicant                   | Primary purpose                           |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| F-Series (e.g., F31, F32) | Predoctoral / postdoctoral fellows | Rigorous training and mentorship          |
| K-Series (e.g., K99/R00)  | Early career investigators         | Transition to research independence       |
| R-Series (e.g., R01)      | Independent investigators          | Long-term health-related research funding |

**Source:**

- [NIH Funding: NIH Grants Fundamentals and Grants Process Playlists](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=NIH+Grants+Fundamentals+Grants+Process+Playlist)

**14. Strategic Grant Writing for Career Independence**

- [ ] 🟧 Treat grants as persuasive pitches for future discovery, not retrospective reports.
- [ ] 🟧 Draft Specific Aims: hook, unmet need, central hypothesis, 2–3 independent aims.
- [ ] 🟧 Build Research Strategy with preliminary data, budget justification, and timelines.
- [ ] 🟧 Navigate resubmissions: interpret Summary Statements and write point-by-point Introductions.
- [ ] 🟧 Avoid over-reliance on AI for scientific rationale generation.

| Grant component        | Strategic function                    | Common failure point                                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Specific Aims page     | Executive summary and persuasive hook | Aims too interdependent (one failure collapses grant)      |
| Significance           | Proves field-moving impact            | Interesting niche with no real-world impact                |
| Approach (methodology) | Proves technical feasibility          | No preliminary data or alternative strategies for pitfalls |

**Source:**

- [Sarah Dobson: Research Grant Writing Fundamentals](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Sarah+Dobson+Research+Grant+Writing+Fundamentals+NIH)

## Autonomous execution framework

Theoretical acquisition must be bound to a rigid operational framework. The transition from novice to expert requires systematic daily exposure, spaced repetition, and active output generation.

### Daily micro-routine (2.5 to 3.5 hours)

| Time of day | Cognitive phase                                             | Required actions and protocols                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Morning     | Epistemological and literature ingestion (45–60 min)        | Consume one peer-reviewed article. Extract bibliographic data into Zotero. Identify study design and FAIR adherence. Draft a 150-word critical summary on methodological flaws. |
| Afternoon   | Methodological and programmatic execution (60–90 min)       | Consume one technical video (R or statistics). Immediately write R syntax on an open-source dataset. Never watch a programming lecture without an active IDE.                   |
| Evening     | Structural deconstruction and rhetorical output (45–60 min) | Deconstruct a highly cited manuscript: nominalizations and funnel structure. Rewrite a poorly constructed abstract using stylistic nuance principles.                           |

### Weekly macro-routine (thematic cycles and peer simulation)

| Days               | Thematic focus                            | Operational objectives                                                                                                      |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Monday – Wednesday | Acquisition & theory                      | Consume new frameworks. Watch designated playlists, take structured notes, update knowledge repositories.                   |
| Thursday – Friday  | Data synthesis & drafting                 | Analyze a dataset in R, generate a publication-quality figure, draft Results and Methods in IMRaD format.                   |
| Saturday           | Simulation & peer review                  | Two-hour Litmaps literature review. Draft a one-page Specific Aims document. Peer or AI review against NIH scoring rubrics. |
| Sunday             | Consolidation & bibliographic maintenance | Maintain Zotero metadata and tags. Review weekly output for conceptual bottlenecks. Suspend active writing.                 |
