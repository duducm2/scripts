## Best Way to Fall Asleep Faster

Use stimulus control from CBT-I. It is the most consistently effective, evidence-based method to shorten sleep-onset time by training your brain to associate bed with sleep only.  
Sources: PMC

### Do This Tonight

- Go to bed only when sleepy, not just tired. If you are not drowsy, stay out of bed.  
  Source: PMC
- Reserve the bed for sleep and sex only. Avoid phones, TV, reading, or planning in bed.  
  Source: PMC
- If you are not asleep after about 15–20 minutes, get up and do a quiet, dim-light activity. Return only when sleepy and repeat as needed.  
  Source: PMC
- Wake up at the same time every day, and set alarms only for wake time. Naps are exceptions, not habits.  
  Source: PMC

Stimulus control produces meaningful reductions in sleep-onset latency versus waitlist or placebo, with effects comparable to other active treatments.  
Sources: PubMed

### If You Often Take More Than 30 Minutes to Fall Asleep

- Run a 7-day “sleep-reset” using sleep restriction.
- Calculate your average actual sleep (example: 5.5 hours). For one week, restrict time in bed to that amount plus about 30 minutes, keeping a fixed wake time.
- Increase time in bed by 15–30 minutes every few days once sleep efficiency exceeds roughly 85%.
- Expect temporary evening sleepiness the first few days; this method rapidly consolidates sleep and shortens sleep-onset time.  
  Sources: JCSM, PubMed, Wiley Online Library

### Fast Add-Ons for Tonight

- Spend 10 minutes on progressive muscle relaxation in dim light just before bed to reduce arousal and improve sleep depth.  
  Source: PMC
- Get bright light soon after waking; keep the last 1–2 hours before bed dim and avoid bright evening light that can delay melatonin.  
  Sources: PMC, Nature
- Avoid caffeine at least 8–10 hours before bed; even moderate doses increase sleep-onset latency and reduce total sleep.  
  Source: OUP Academic
- Maintain daytime movement; regular aerobic exercise improves sleep quality and reduces time to fall asleep, especially earlier in the day.  
  Source: BJP

### When to Escalate care

- If trouble falling asleep occurs three or more nights per week for three or more months, pursue CBT-I with a trained clinician or digital program; it outperforms medications long-term.  
  Source: AASM
