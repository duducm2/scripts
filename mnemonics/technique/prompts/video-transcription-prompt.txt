## Output contract

Produce **one Markdown document** following this exact skeleton, top to bottom:

```md
# <Topic title of the video>

**Source:** <YouTube URL>
**Language(s):** <e.g. English, or "German with English gloss">

## Overview

<2–4 sentences summarizing the video's scope and purpose.>

## <Concept 1 title>

<Short polished explanation in full sentences (2–5 sentences). Preserve depth; remove repetition.>

> "<verbatim sentence from the video that defines or exemplifies this concept>"

**Visual/auditory cue:** <e.g. "The instructor points to the whiteboard where the formula is written." — omit line if none.>

**Examples:**

- <Concrete example 1 from the video, with any verbatim example sentences preserved>
- <Concrete example 2>

## <Concept 2 title>

...

## Exact sentences for story reuse

- "<verbatim sentence 1>" — <concept it belongs to>
- "<verbatim sentence 2>" — <concept it belongs to>
- ...
```

**Formatting rules:**

- Save output as `.md` — no code fences wrapping the entire document.
- Use `>` blockquote syntax for verbatim quotes so downstream tools can scan them.
- The `## Exact sentences for story reuse` section MUST include at least one verbatim quote per concept in the transcript.
- Do not add tables of contents, disclaimers, or meta-commentary about the transcription process.

---

## Procedure

### 1. Watch and extract

- Process the entire video. Do not skip sections.
- Extract every distinct concept, definition, rule, and example. Do not omit significant details.
- Remove repetition (repeated phrases, filler, redundant restatements), but strictly preserve **content depth** so downstream story generation has full semantic material.

### 2. Preserve verbatim sentences

- For each key concept or rule, include at least one **exact quote** or near-verbatim sentence from the video.
- Preserve grammar examples, formula statements, and rule statements exactly as spoken or shown on screen.
- Quotes MUST match transcript audio or on-screen text word-for-word. NEVER paraphrase, "clean up", or shorten a quote.

### 3. Note visual and auditory cues

- Where the video reinforces a concept with a visual (on-screen text, diagram, instructor gesture) or auditory (emphasis, tone shift, repetition), add a `**Visual/auditory cue:**` line under that concept.
- These cues let the downstream Architect wire mnemonic beats to concrete video moments.

### 4. Language handling

- **English videos:** transcribe in English.
- **Non-English videos:** transcribe in the source language. For each concept, additionally add an **English gloss** in parentheses after the verbatim source-language quote so the Architect can work in either language.
- **German content (any video containing German words or phrases):** whenever a German word or short fixed German phrase (about four words or fewer) appears in your **authored prose** (overview, concept explanations, cue lines), append IPA in **square brackets** immediately following, with a single space before the opening bracket. Use standard German (Hochdeutsch) IPA, broad transcription, with a stress mark ˈ before the stressed syllable.
  - Example: `The sign reads verboten [fɛɐ̯ˈboːtn̩].`
  - Example (short phrase): `Guten Morgen [ˈɡuːtn̩ ˈmɔʁɡn̩].`
- **Inside verbatim quotes:** NEVER insert IPA into the quoted characters (quotes stay identical to source). Instead, immediately **after** the quote line, add one line starting with `IPA:` listing every distinct German word or short phrase from that quote in the same bracket style, comma-separated if several.
  - Example line: `IPA: verboten [fɛɐ̯ˈboːtn̩], Schadenfreude [ˈʃaːdn̩ˌfʁɔʏdə]`

### 5. Style

- Polished, cohesive, concise, coherent. Full sentences where they help clarity.
- Plain vocabulary. Prefer short everyday words in your own prose; keep discipline jargon only when it appears inside a preserved quote.
- Optimize for **downstream reuse**: the Story Architect will scan your `>` blockquotes and your `## Exact sentences for story reuse` list to build mnemonic beats. Make both scannable.

### Common failure modes (NEVER do these)

- NEVER paraphrase a video sentence and label it as a quote.
- NEVER omit a concept because it "seems obvious" — the Architect needs full coverage.
- NEVER wrap the whole document in a single code fence — output raw Markdown.
- NEVER add meta commentary ("As requested, here is the transcript…").
- NEVER drop visual/auditory cues that reinforce key concepts.
- NEVER modify a German quote to insert IPA inside it — use the separate `IPA:` line.
